Confidence Grows in Community
How encouragement helps young people find their voice

A young person’s confidence often grows in ordinary moments: being welcomed by name, invited to share an idea, and listened to without being rushed.

CTYBC JOURNALREFLECTION & PRACTICAL GUIDANCE
Belonging gives children and teenagers room to practise. In a supportive group, they can speak up, make mistakes, try again and learn that their contribution matters. Encouragement works best when it is specific: “You explained that clearly,” or “I appreciate how you included someone else.”

Adults can help by setting a tone of respect. Listen before correcting. Ask questions that invite thought. Give different young people chances to lead a prayer, read a passage, welcome a visitor or help plan an activity. Quiet participation also deserves recognition; leadership can begin with careful listening.

Put it into practice

A healthy community does not expect every young person to be outgoing. It makes space for different personalities while giving each person a meaningful way to take part. James 1:19 offers a useful posture for everyone in the room: be quick to listen and slow to speak.

With time, steady encouragement helps young people carry a stronger sense of worth into school, friendships, church and family life.

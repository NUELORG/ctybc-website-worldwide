Make Room for Honest Questions
Listening well when teenagers are working through faith

Questions are part of growing. When teenagers bring difficult or unfinished thoughts, a calm response can keep the conversation open.

CTYBC JOURNALREFLECTION & PRACTICAL GUIDANCE
The first response matters. Instead of rushing to correct or dismiss, try listening fully and asking what prompted the question. A young person may be looking for information, reassurance, or simply a safe person who will take them seriously.

Adults do not need to pretend to know everything. It is honest to say, “I’m not sure; let’s look into it together.” Choose trustworthy sources, read relevant passages in context, and return to the conversation. If a question involves safety, abuse or serious distress, involve an appropriate trusted adult or professional promptly.

Put it into practice

Respectful discussion does not mean every answer will be easy. It does mean the young person is treated with care while they think. James 1:19 reminds listeners to slow down and make room for another person’s words.

A patient conversation can build trust. It tells teenagers that faith can be explored thoughtfully and that asking for help is welcome.

The Courage to Begin Again
Small steps after a difficult season

Setbacks can make a young person feel that progress has disappeared. A patient reminder can help them see that one hard moment does not define the whole story.

CTYBC JOURNALREFLECTION & PRACTICAL GUIDANCE
When something goes wrong, begin with care rather than a lecture. Name what happened without turning it into a label about who the young person is. Ask what support would help now, and separate what can be repaired from what needs time.

The next step can be modest: apologise, ask for help, make a new plan or try once more with guidance. Adults can share their own learning carefully, without taking over the young person’s experience. When the situation is serious, seek the right support and do not ask a child to carry it alone.

Put it into practice

Galatians 6:9 encourages perseverance in doing good. For young people, perseverance is easier when they have people who notice effort, offer practical help and celebrate steady progress rather than demanding instant success.

Beginning again is not pretending the difficulty never happened. It is choosing, with support, one constructive step toward what comes next.
